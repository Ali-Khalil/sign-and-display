Choose the size of a shop signboard from the distance it will be read from, not from the largest size that fits. A widely used rule is one inch of letter height for every ten feet of viewing distance. The board is then sized to hold those letters with clear space around them.

## How far away will people read the sign?

Stand where your customers first see the shop: across the road, at the corner, or from a car. Estimate that distance. A sign on a narrow market lane is read from a few steps away; a sign on a main road is read from much further.

## What letter height do I need?

The table applies the one-inch-per-ten-feet rule. Treat it as a starting point; bold, simple letters with good contrast can be read from further.

| Viewing distance | Letter height |
|---|---|
| 30 ft | 3 in |
| 60 ft | 6 in |
| 100 ft | 10 in |
| 150 ft | 15 in |
| 200 ft | 20 in |

## How much space should there be around the letters?

Leave clear space on all sides so the name does not look squeezed. As a rough guide, the letters should take up about two thirds of the board's height and stop well short of each end. A second line, such as a phone number, should be clearly smaller than the name.

## How do I measure my shopfront?

1. Measure the width of the space above the shop where the sign will go.
2. Measure the height available between the top of the shutter or door and the floor above.
3. Note anything in the way: air-conditioner units, pipes, cables or a neighbour's sign.
4. Take a straight-on photo from across the road.
